Dates We Remember

Holocaust remembrance, the history of Israel and Italian Jewish life throughout the year.

Israel’s observances follow the Hebrew calendar and fall on a different civil date each year. For each one we give the next date, and the holidays are listed in the Jewish calendar.

  1. January 27

    Holocaust Remembrance Day

    On January 27, 1945, the Red Army entered Auschwitz. Italy remembers the Holocaust and the persecutions under Law 211 of 2000.

  2. March 6

    Day of the Righteous

    Established by the European Parliament in 2012 and recognized in Italy by Law 212 of 2017. It honors those who stood up to genocide and saved lives at the risk of their own.

  3. April 25

    Liberation Day and the Jewish Brigade

    In 1945 the Jewish Brigade, made up of volunteers from Mandatory Palestine serving in the British Army, fought on the Italian front. On April 25, Italy’s Liberation Day, we also remember its contribution.

  4. 27 Nisan

    Yom HaShoah

    יום השואה

    Holocaust and Heroism Remembrance Day

    The date falls close to the anniversary of the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising, which began in 1943. In Israel, at ten in the morning, sirens sound for two minutes and the whole country comes to a standstill.

  5. 4 Iyar

    Yom HaZikaron

    יום הזיכרון

    Memorial Day for Israel’s Fallen Soldiers and Victims of Terrorism

    Israel remembers its fallen soldiers and the victims of terrorism. At sundown, mourning gives way to the celebration of Independence Day.

  6. 5 Iyar

    Yom HaAtzmaut

    יום העצמאות

    Israel Independence Day

    Independence Day according to the Hebrew calendar. It marks the proclamation of the State on 5 Iyar 5708.

  7. May 14

    Proclamation of the State of Israel, 1948

    On May 14, 1948, in Tel Aviv, David Ben-Gurion read the Declaration of Independence. It is the civil date of the birth of the State of Israel.

  8. 28 Iyar

    Yom Yerushalayim

    יום ירושלים

    Jerusalem Day

    It marks the reunification of Jerusalem in June 1967, during the Six-Day War. After nineteen years, Jews returned to pray at the Western Wall.

  9. September

    European Day of Jewish Culture

    On a Sunday in September, synagogues, museums and Jewish sites open to the public across Europe. In Italy it is coordinated by the Union of Italian Jewish Communities.

  10. September 5 · November 17

    Racial Laws, 1938

    On September 5, 1938, Jews were expelled from Italian schools, and on November 17 the measures “for the defense of the race” followed. From 1943, the persecution of rights became the persecution of lives.

  11. October 7

    October 7, 2023 Massacre

    Hamas killed about 1,200 people in Israel and abducted 251. No day since the end of the Holocaust had seen so many Jews killed.

  12. October 9

    Attack on the Great Synagogue of Rome, 1982

    A Palestinian commando attacked worshippers leaving the Great Synagogue on Shemini Atzeret. Two-year-old Stefano Gaj Taché was killed, and dozens of people were wounded.

  13. October 16

    Roundup of the Rome Ghetto, 1943

    The SS arrested more than a thousand Roman Jews and deported them to Auschwitz. Sixteen came back.

  14. November 9

    Kristallnacht, 1938

    During the night of November 9–10, synagogues, shops and Jewish homes were destroyed across Germany and Austria. Thousands of people were arrested and sent to camps.

  15. November 29

    UN Partition Vote, 1947

    The UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 181 with the plan to partition Mandatory Palestine. It paved the way for the birth of the State of Israel.
